Consensus Adjustment

How to use this
Pick a SKU, try each forecast method, and watch the Best Fit badge — it's picking the method with the lowest in-sample MAPE, the way an auto-model-selection engine would. Tune α/β/γ to see bias vs. variance trade-offs. Then use Advance 1 month to lock in a real call and check it against next month's actual, like a live S&OP cycle.

⚠ Inventory Risk

How to read this
EOQ is the order quantity that minimises ordering + holding cost together (Wilson formula). Safety stock covers demand variability during lead time at your chosen service level. Reorder point = expected lead-time demand + safety stock — place the PO the moment on-hand position drops to this line. Raise the ordering-cost slider to see EOQ grow (fewer, bigger orders); raise holding-cost % to see it shrink.

🔻 Lowest Perfect-Order SKUs

How to read this
Fill Rate = % of ordered units shipped from stock. OTIF = % of orders delivered on-time AND in-full — the stricter, combined metric most S&OP reviews actually track. Perfect Order layers in a quality/accuracy factor on top of OTIF. Turns/yr comes straight from the Inventory module's current policy sliders — tune ordering/holding cost there and this scorecard updates too.

📊 Scenario Comparison — Network Revenue at Risk

How to read this
The baseline is each SKU's best-fit statistical forecast (same engine as the Demand module). The scenario % layers a sales/consensus adjustment on top — this is what a real S&OP cycle does after the statistical forecast comes out of Demand Review. Capacity is your supply plan; wherever demand exceeds it, that's unmet demand — the gap Supply Review and Reconciliation exist to close, either by raising capacity, trimming the scenario, or accepting the revenue risk.
